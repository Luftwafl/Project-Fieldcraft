BETA 0.4.0 PRE-RELEASE 17 - MAIN MAP

Accounts, PVE and PVP, death to the role menu (0.4.0 pre-release 17). ACCOUNT on the main menu lets you create an account or log in. Your cash, owned scopes, equipped scope and stats are saved to the account on the server, so they follow you to another browser. Progress made while logged out stays in that browser only. PLAY now asks for PVE (single player against AI squads) or PVP (against other people). PVP needs a login, then finds the server with the most players that still has room and takes you to team and role selection for it. Dying no longer respawns you straight away: you go back to the role menu, with a note that you were killed, and deploy again from there. In PVP you stay on the same server. The GUNSMITH button is on the role menu. Limits: passwords are stored as hashes on the server, but the server runs over plain HTTP unless you put it behind HTTPS, so do not reuse an important password. Only one login is active per account at a time: logging in elsewhere signs out the other browser. Only the wallet is saved to the account; settings are still per browser. Admins are the account names in the server's admin list, so the owner should register those names first.

Full map, Gunsmith, multiplayer and conquest (0.4.0 pre-release 17). Hold M (or tap the minimap, or use the full-map button) to open a full-screen map. It shows the capture points with their owners and progress, the bases, your team's living soldiers (the team leader is marked gold) and your vehicle, and it stays open without pausing the game. Enemy positions are not shown. The Gunsmith on the home screen lets you buy scopes with money earned from kills ($100), captures ($200), match wins ($1000) and match losses ($250). Iron sights are free, the red dot costs $400, the 3x scope $900 and the 6x scope $1800. Scopes apply a zoom to the primary weapon when aimed, with a reticle overlay. They are an overlay, not a model attachment. Money is saved per browser and is not shared. Multiplayer: the server also serves the game, so open the server's address in a browser (for example http://your-ip:8080), go to PLAY, MULTIPLAYER, click Refresh, pick a server, choose a team and role, and deploy. Servers are run by whoever hosts server.js and are not reachable from the sandbox. Multiplayer is human-only (no NPC sync), and the server relays what clients report about their shots, hits and deaths without checking them, so it is not cheat-proof. Admins are players whose name is in the server's admin list; they can kick players and restart the match. If no admin key is set, anyone who types an admin name gets admin rights. Conquest is the multiplayer and single-player mode: five villages are the capture points; a point flips after 12 seconds of one team holding it alone; tickets fall while a team holds fewer points; a team wins at zero tickets or by holding all points for 20 seconds. Limits: multiplayer was tested with two browser clients on one machine; remote player facing was not verified visually, and phone play was not tested.

New menus (0.4.0 pre-release 17). The game now opens on a main menu with PLAY, CONTROLS, GRAPHICS, AUDIO and WHAT'S NEW, and a Now Playing card. PLAY opens SELECT TEAM (NATO or WARSAW PACT), then SELECT ROLE: Rifleman, Assault or Support, with a loadout panel and a DEPLOY AS button. Back buttons return one screen at a time, and the field menu's Main menu returns here. Limits: STORE, PROFILE, RECORDS, CODES and spawn-on-ally are not in this build. Role limits are shown but not enforced, and the menu shows no slot counts.

NPC movement, engagement and squads (0.4.0 pre-release 17). Patrol leaders stall-check by net movement: a leader that has gone less than 1.5 m in 4 s replans, and after three replans it takes the next route point. Each squad now picks its own lane positions and jitter, so two squads no longer share the same destinations. Patrol squads hold at village objectives for about 35 to 70 s before moving on. Opposing soldiers outside the close-range tier now sense each other and open fire at long range, and distant fights are resolved statistically. Gunfire within 60 m is heard: a listener goes suspicious and investigates the sound, without seeing the shooter. An investigation that finds nothing returns to routine.

Truck spawn and driving (0.4.0 pre-release 17). Each truck spawn point now has a large yellow TRUCK SPAWN sign on two posts, with a tall yellow beacon mast beside it that can be seen from about 120 m away. The trigger pad has the same marker, a little smaller. A flat yellow outline on the ground shows where the truck appears. Trucks now steer like vehicles: the wheel swings to about 29 degrees at a limited rate and returns to centre when released, the heading changes at a rate set by speed and steering, so a stopped truck cannot turn on the spot, braking slows it at about 7 m/s², engine drag slows it when no throttle is held, and reverse is limited to 4 m/s with the steering reversed.

Limits. Distant skirmishes are statistical, not simulated shot by shot. Civilians do not respawn. Mates who can also see a target cannot be separated from mates told about it. Thin walls can be missed by line-of-sight sampling. Stall recovery and the destination check were run in one desktop test browser only; the phone and Chromebook were not tested. Truck controls were checked with a fixed-step simulation through the game's own truck code, not with live keyboard timing, and touch steering was not tested on a device. The trucks still appear about 15 m from the pad.

Patrols, respawns, awareness and squads (0.4.0 pre-release 16). Patrols now move out. Units more than 220 m from the player were frozen, so the far squads never left their base; they now keep moving on their routes at a low rate, without being drawn. Squad leaders steer along a real path through gates and around walls, not in straight lines, so they no longer stay in their base compound. A stalled leader replans; a point it cannot reach is skipped.

Respawn. A dead soldier's body is removed after 12 s. Its squad slot is refilled 30 s after the death, at a free spawn point at the base, up to eight per side. Checked over three kill waves: every slot refilled, no duplicates, no orphaned models.

Awareness and aiming. Soldiers move from unaware to suspicious (investigating), then to alert after a reaction delay of about 0.5 to 2 s, then to engaged. Awareness builds over time: faster when the target is near, moving or standing; slower when the player is crouched or prone. A soldier aims at the last place it saw the target, not the live position, and drops the target after about 6 s without sight. Each soldier has its own skill: hit chance falls with range, against moving or prone targets, and under fire, and misses land close.

Squads. A confirmed contact is passed to squad mates within 60 m. If a leader is lost, the next soldier in the squad takes over the route and the rest keep formation.

Truck spawn sign. A TRUCK SPAWN sign now also stands beside each truck trigger pad, the yellow ring that spawns the trucks. The sign at the truck spawn point is kept.

Limits. Civilians do not respawn. Mates who can also see a target themselves cannot be separated from mates told about it. Thin walls can be missed by the line-of-sight sampling; solid walls are caught. Return to routine after an investigation and stall recovery were checked indirectly. Timing was measured in a desktop test browser only, not on a phone. The trucks still appear about 15 m from the pad.

More than one key per action, and an easier way out of this log (0.4.0 pre-release 15). Each action can now have up to six keys or mouse buttons, like Minecraft keybinds. Open Controls, press + on an action, then press the new key or mouse button. Press × to remove an extra key. If a key is already used, the prompt offers SWAP (the two actions exchange keys) or MOVE (the key moves from its other action), or CANCEL. Saved controls from earlier builds still load. Restore defaults clears the extra keys. The changelog has a Back button at the top as well as at the bottom, and Escape also leaves it.

Truck spawn sign (0.4.0 pre-release 15). A yellow TRUCK SPAWN sign on posts stands 7 m beside each truck spawn point, on the side away from the road, so it does not block the road or a spawning truck. It has no collision. Checked by scripted test and one screenshot at 12 m.

Limits. The AI, patrol, respawn and squad-coordination work in the pre-release 15 spec is not in this build yet. Soldiers still do not respawn after they die, and patrol and awareness behaviour is unchanged from pre-release 14. The sign text is drawn on a flat panel, so it reads best from the front. Checked with scripted tests listed in the test report.

Soldier uniforms match the player (0.4.0 pre-release 14). Soldiers now use their faction's uniform, sleeve, webbing and helmet colours, taken from the same table as the player's kit. Each soldier carries the weapon its faction issues for its class, so Assault soldiers have the machine gun or light machine gun, and Riflemen and Support soldiers carry the rifle. Each class has its own kit: riflemen wear ammunition pouches, assault soldiers a pack, and support soldiers a radio on the back. Squad leaders are riflemen. Civilians flee more reliably: they turn around obstacles instead of stopping.

Limits. Models are still boxes. Soldier weapons are simplified shapes, not the first-person weapon models. Soldiers do not use the player's pistol. Class limits are not enforced for NPC squads. Checked with scripted tests and one screenshot.

Squads, combat, and more people (0.4.0 pre-release 13). Each side has two squads of four: a leader patrols routes between its base and village lanes, and the other three follow in formation. Squads on the other side are hostile. Soldiers acquire a target by sight, fire with a distance-based hit chance, leave tracers and make the rifle sound. A soldier that is hit goes to cover if there is a hiding spot nearby. Soldiers throw grenades at you from 14 to 40 m, with a 6 m blast radius. Your squad fights the enemy but does not fire on you. Thirty civilians walk village lanes and flee gunfire. A killed character falls over and sinks slightly. Characters near you have a detailed model: helmet, vest, rifle, arms and legs that swing as they walk, and an aim pose when alert.

Fixes. Soldiers used to stay on alert forever after losing sight of you. They now return to patrol after a short memory. Units that slide along a wall for 3 s take a new nearby waypoint. Units now step around fences and walls instead of stopping at them.

Limits. There is no pathfinding: routes are straight lines, and units slide around obstacles. Characters walk through trees, which are not collision for characters. Cover exists only where there is an obstacle; open ground has none. Grenades fly straight and do not bounce. Enemy fire has no spatial sound, only the rifle sound at a fixed volume. Models are boxes with pivoted limbs; there are no textures, no facial detail, and no ragdoll physics. The fall is a scripted tilt. In tests, one squad leader stayed near its base in some runs. Soldier-against-soldier fights were not tested directly.

Characters, combat and health (0.4.0 pre-release 12). Two civilians walk each village lane and flee when shot at or approached. Four enemy soldiers patrol the enemy base. A soldier that sees you within 85 m, inside a 140-degree cone and with a clear line of sight goes on alert and fires at up to 120 m. The chance of a hit falls with distance. A hit costs 18 HP. You start with 100 HP, shown in the top bar. At 0 HP you respawn at your base with full health. Your rounds hit soldiers and civilians: head 100 damage, body 40, legs 25.

Simulation levels. Characters closer than 90 m update every frame and can see and fire. Between 90 and 220 m they update 4 times a second and only patrol. Beyond 220 m they are hidden and not updated. Numbers are fixed, so all quality presets have the same characters.

Fixes. Lean toggle now records a key press even if it starts and ends between two frames, so a quick tap is not lost. Doors on villages along the x axis now face their street (see pre-release 11).

Limits. Soldier AI has no cover, no squad tactics, no friendly soldiers, and no grenades. Enemy fire has no sound and no tracers. Characters are simple box models with no animation. Death is a fall, not a ragdoll. Civilians do not react beyond fleeing. Performance is measured as JavaScript time in headless Chromium only: about 0.01 ms per frame near the player with 14 characters. GPU cost, real hardware and phone touch are not measured. Scenario tests are 12 scripted checks; the damage check is statistical because hits are random.

Town square, workshops, farm interiors and an extra connector (0.4.0 pre-release 11). The largest villages have a paved square with a well and two benches, cleared of homes within 12 m of the centre. Mid-size and large villages have a workshop in place of one home, with a workbench, vice, pegboard of tools and crates. Barns and farm buildings have a cart and sacks. The one village that had no road connector now has a two-part track with a bend.

Fix. Doors on villages laid out along the x axis faced away from the street. All 15 village homes now face their street.

Limits. Checked with scripted tests and two interior screenshots. The farm interior screenshot was too dark to confirm. The elbow connector was checked for clearance and road contact only, not visually. Walking into the new counters, benches and workbenches is not tested. Building families are shop, workshop, home, barn and farm; no other families yet. No interiors beyond the shop, workshop and barn furniture. Civilian NPCs, soldier AI, simulation levels and the performance pass are not started.

Shop interior (0.4.0 pre-release 10). The village shops now have a counter across the room from the door and two shelving units on the side walls. They are set to block movement, but walking into them is not yet tested. Checked with a scripted interior screenshot only.

Limits. Shops are the only building type with a new interior. Workshops and farm buildings still have the earlier furniture. No town square yet. The one village without a connector is unchanged. Civilian NPCs, soldier AI, simulation levels and the performance pass are not started.

Vegetation (0.4.0 pre-release 9). Forests now vary more. Broadleaf trees are more common at the forest edge and in some patches, and conifers dominate the dense core. Each tree has its own width and height, so silhouettes differ. Some glades are opened up inside forests. Ferns grow in the forest interior, and fallen logs lie on the ground. Trees, ferns and logs are all kept off roads, villages and connector tracks. Counts are fixed, so all three quality presets still show the same world.

Limits. Forests are still fairly open, and the ferns and logs are small against the terrain. Not separately measured: the exact tree count after the clearings. The fairness check passes, but no hardware or phone check was done.

Village roads (0.4.0 pre-release 8). Four of the five villages now have a dirt track from the end of their street to the nearest point of the existing road network. The track keeps at least 5 m clear of homes, and trees are kept off it. One village has no connector because no route met the clearance rule.

Environment still not finished. Not yet built: the forest ecosystem (species mix, clearings and forest edges beyond the current tree placement), distinct interiors for shops, workshops and farm buildings, more building families beyond size, window count and colour, and a town square. Civilians, soldiers and the simulation levels are not started.

Limits. Connectors are straight tracks, not routed roads. They are not part of the road network for driving speed or the road-distance checks. Checked with scripted tests and screenshots only.

The minimap shows both trucks when they are not being driven. Limits: no damage, fuel, passengers or suspension.

Furniture. Wardrobes, beds, tables, stoves and shelves block movement; chairs, rugs and pictures do not. The doorway stays clear. Limits: houses are still single-storey, with no curtains, lights, electricity or upper floors. Building positions moved slightly from 0.3.75.

— Beta 0.3.0 —

Battlefield. The map is about 760 m square with broader hills and valleys. NATO main base is in the north-west and Warsaw Pact main base in the south-east, about 550 m apart. Five villages (Hrazany, Lipova, Sedlec, Dubrava, Ostrov) are spread across the map, with houses, barns and fields.

Roads. A paved main route links the NATO base, Hrazany, Sedlec and the Warsaw Pact base. Dirt roads and tracks give alternative routes through the centre and south. Roads follow the terrain. Off-road movement is still possible.

Outposts and resupply. Four small outposts (sandbag lines, a tent and crates) sit on the approaches. Each main base and outpost has a green resupply ring. Stand still inside the ring for about a second to refill every weapon's reserve. Ammunition is unlimited.

— Beta 0.2.5 and earlier —

Weapons. NATO: M16A2 (semi and full auto, no burst), M60E3 belt-fed machine gun, Beretta M9. Warsaw Pact: AK-74, RPK-74 magazine-fed machine gun, Makarov PM. Obsolete M16A1, XM177E2, M1911A1, AKM and AKMS removed.

Ballistics. Rounds now travel with gravity drop and are checked along their path, so thin objects are not skipped at high speed.

Penetration. Foliage does not stop rounds. Thin wood, fabric and sheet metal can be penetrated while the round keeps enough energy. Sandbags, rock, concrete, walls and tree trunks stop rounds.

Feedback. Impact sparks and dust differ by material, with entry and exit marks on penetration. Rounds are drawn as short tracers.

Uniforms. NATO: woodland-pattern BDU with a PASGT-style helmet and M1956-era pouches. Warsaw Pact: khaki-olive tunic with a stand-up collar and an SSh-68-style helmet with flared rim.

Fixes. M60E3 and RPK-74 now fire fully automatic when fire is held. Rounds are drawn as tracers, so bullet drop is visible: about 6 cm at 100 m and 50 cm at 300 m.

Known limits. The woodland pattern is procedural, not a reproduction of the M81 print. Helmet shapes are simplified. The Beretta M9 uses the 1911 model. The belt-fed M60E3 reload uses the magazine animation. Targets remain binary, because there is no health or damage system yet. The Warsaw Pact base sits in a basin: from its spawn, standing sightlines are about 15–60 m before hills or base structures block them.
